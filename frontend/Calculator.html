Personal Income Tax (PIT)

PIT is levied on the income of individuals from various sources including employment, business, investment, etc.

  • Standard rate applies to most salaried employees
  • Tax relief and deductions reduce your taxable income
  • Different states may have varying rates
  • Minimum tax provisions apply to businesses

Company Income Tax (CIT)

CIT applies to the profit of companies and other corporate entities.

  • Standard rate: 30% for most companies
  • SME rate: Reduced rate for small businesses
  • Education Development Levy: Separate from main CIT
  • Deductible expenses reduce taxable profit

Value Added Tax (VAT)

VAT is a consumption tax charged on the supply of goods and services.

  • Standard VAT rate: 7.5%
  • Some supplies are VAT-exempt
  • Exports are typically zero-rated
  • Registered businesses can claim input VAT

What is the difference between gross and taxable income?

Gross income is your total earnings. Taxable income is what remains after deducting allowable reliefs, deductions, and personal allowance. Tax is calculated on the taxable income.

Can I claim tax deductions?

Yes, you can claim allowable deductions such as professional fees, business expenses, capital allowances (for businesses), and approved reliefs. Keep proper documentation to support your claims.

What is VAT input tax?

VAT input tax is the VAT you pay on goods and services you purchase for your business. If you're VAT-registered, you can usually claim this back, paying only the difference between output VAT (on sales) and input VAT.

When should I register for VAT?

In Nigeria, businesses with annual turnover exceeding ₦25 million must register for VAT. You can voluntarily register even with lower turnover.

What penalties apply for late tax payment?

Late payment penalties typically range from 5-10% depending on how overdue the payment is. Additional interest may apply. It's important to file and pay taxes on time.

Where can I get professional tax advice?

You can consult with chartered accountants, tax practitioners, or visit the official FIRS (Federal Inland Revenue Service) website for authoritative guidance.
